About the Game

Arrow Simulator is a simple side scroller where you control an arrow that collects valueable icons to sell in a shop. Upgrades can be purchased to allow your arrow to move faster and pick up collectibles from further away. The collectibles can be upgraded as well, to increase their sell value. Unlocking new collectibles also triggers more difficult obstacles. The game has a humorous tutorial, which is actually less of a tutorial and more an integral part of the game's progression.

The Developer

Gavin Ray is a solo-indie developer located in the Pacific Northwest, USA. He is a big fan of indie games, especially small projects that introduce experimental mechanics. In his own work, he is making a collection of short games that each relate to some controversial aspect of real life. For example, the first of this series, Pal-Mon, is about how big game companies will secure patents over common game mechanics, and then use the patents to go after smaller developers. Think Nintendo v. PocketPair.

Inspiration

The inspiration for Arrow Simulator came from Mateo Covic's viral conversation about Steam's refund policy. The question it attempts to answer is, how can a solo developer create a game that is short and to the point, but still takes at least 2 hours to play? Some players will speed run, while others will afk immediately upon opening the game. Arrow Simulator attempts to solve this issue in a unique and humorous way.
